What the stages, RS and levels on this site mean

Stage 1 to 4

Every stock is placed in one of four stages from its daily price and its 50, 150 and 200 day averages (DMA). The idea comes from stage analysis: a stock moves from resting, to rising, to topping, to falling, and then repeats.

StageNameWhat it meansRule used on this site
Stage 1BaseResting or moving sideways. Neither buyers nor sellers are in control.Anything that is not Stage 2, 3 or 4.
Stage 2AdvancingA healthy uptrend. This is the stage most trend followers look at.All of these together: price above the 50, 150 and 200 DMA · 50 DMA above 150 DMA · 150 DMA above 200 DMA · 200 DMA rising versus 20 days ago · price within 25% of its 52 week high · price at least 30% above its 52 week low.
Stage 3ToppingThe uptrend is losing strength. Price slips below the short average but has not broken down yet.Not Stage 2 or 4, price below the 50 DMA, and still above 95% of the 200 DMA.
Stage 4DecliningA downtrend. Price is weak and the long average is falling.Price below the 200 DMA, 200 DMA not rising, and 50 DMA below the 200 DMA.
NewNew listingToo new for the stage to be worked out.Fewer than 200 trading days of price history. Stage, RS and 52 week levels are not calculated.

The stage is only a description of the chart. In the Backtest page, being in Stage 2 mattered more than any single price level, but it still does not predict the next day.

Other numbers

RS (relative strength)A score from 1 to 99. It ranks the stock's price change over the last 3, 6, 9 and 12 months (weighted 40%, 20%, 20%, 20%) against all other stocks. RS 90 means it did better than about 90% of stocks. Higher is stronger.
Days in Stage 2How many trading days in a row the stock has been in Stage 2. A small number means a fresh Stage 2.
From 52W highHow far the price is below its highest price of the last year.
Avg volumeAverage number of shares traded per day (last 20 days). Low volume stocks are harder to buy and sell.

Price levels

Levels come from a volume profile: the last year of price data is split into price bands and Scope counts how much trading happened in each band.

VLOW · Break downBottom of the range that holds 70% of trading. Price falling below it is a break down.
VHIGH · BreakoutTop of that range. Price moving above it is a breakout.
MagnetThe single price where the most trading happened. Price is often pulled back towards it.

Zones on the Levels page: Above VHIGH, Near VHIGH, Inside range, Near VLOW, Below VLOW.

Open zone

Type VLOW, VHIGH, the magnet and today's opening price. The page shows where the open sits on the ladder and what happened on earlier days that opened in the same place: which nearby level was touched first, how often price came back from it, and how the day ended compared with all days. Only the opening price is used.

New listings

New IPOs show live price and intraday levels only, because there is not enough history for 52 week levels, Stage or RS. Add a symbol to watch.txt on GitHub (one per line) and it shows up within about 15 minutes in market hours.

Everything here is calculated from past prices with fixed rules. It describes charts, it is not advice to buy or sell.
